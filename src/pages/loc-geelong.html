The Market

Geelong is a harder market — and a better one.

Geelong is Victoria's second-largest city, and that cuts both ways for anyone trying to get found online. There are far more businesses competing for the same searches than anywhere along the coast, so ranking takes more sustained work and rarely happens by accident.

The compensation is volume. A decent position for a Geelong service term is worth substantially more traffic than the same position in a small coastal town — enough that the work required to earn it usually pays for itself faster.

Why proximity changes your strategy

Local search results are heavily influenced by how close the searcher is to the business. In Torquay, that's a minor consideration — the town isn't big enough for it to matter much. Across Geelong it matters enormously. Someone searching in Newtown and someone searching in Corio can get completely different results for an identical query.

The practical consequence is that a single page saying "we serve Geelong" competes weakly everywhere. Businesses covering multiple suburbs generally do better with dedicated pages for the areas that genuinely matter to them, each with real content about working in that area — not the same paragraph with the suburb name swapped, which Google identifies as thin duplicate content and largely disregards.

Competing without the biggest budget

You will not out-spend the largest operators in Geelong, and you don't need to. Smaller businesses win local search by being more specific: targeting the services and suburbs the big players treat as an afterthought, keeping a genuinely complete Google Business Profile, and collecting reviews steadily rather than in bursts.

Specificity beats budget more often than people expect. Ranking first for a search that five hundred people make every month, with clear intent to buy, is worth more than ranking tenth for one that fifty thousand people make idly.

Approach

What a Geelong build looks like.

Multi-suburb structure

If you cover Geelong plus surrounding areas, the site is structured with a genuine page per location worth targeting, each with distinct content rather than a template.

Service-level pages

One page listing eight services competes for none of them properly. Separate pages for your highest-value services target the searches that actually convert.

Speed as an advantage

Many established Geelong sites are slow, plugin-heavy builds. A fast, hand-coded site is a real competitive edge — both for rankings and for the people using it.

Reviews as the moat

In a competitive market, a steady stream of genuine reviews is the hardest thing for a competitor to replicate quickly. Every build includes a system for collecting them.
