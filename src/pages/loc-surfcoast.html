The Region

Seasonal business needs a different website.

Trade along this coast swings harder than almost anywhere else in Victoria. A business in Lorne or Anglesea can do a substantial share of its annual revenue between December and Easter, then spend the winter serving a few hundred locals.

Most websites are built as though every month looks the same. That's a genuine missed opportunity in both directions — the site under-performs when demand peaks, and it does nothing to hold local trade when the visitors go home.

Peak season: remove every obstacle

Summer traffic is impatient, mobile and often standing outside deciding between you and whoever else appeared in the search. Everything that matters has to be immediate: current opening hours, an obvious way to book or call, prices if you have them, and a page that loads instantly on a phone with two bars of reception.

Slow loading is especially costly here. Mobile coverage along parts of the coast is genuinely patchy, and a heavy site that's merely sluggish in town can be effectively broken in Aireys Inlet. Building light isn't a nicety in this region.

Off season: hold the locals

The same site needs to work for the person who lives here year-round. That's a different message — reliability, familiarity, being the one who actually answers the phone in July — and it's usually where the Google Business Profile does the heavy lifting, since locals search by name and by map far more than visitors do.

Hours that change, and why they matter

If your hours differ between seasons, they need to be correct in both places: on the site and on your Google profile. Wrong hours are one of the fastest ways to earn a bad review from someone who drove forty minutes to a closed door — and Google actively demotes profiles whose information proves unreliable.
