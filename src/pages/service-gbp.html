Your Google Business Profile is free, and for most local businesses it drives more calls than the website does. It's also the thing most businesses set up once and never touch again.

Get a profile audit →

Search for almost any local service and the first thing you see below the ads is a map with three businesses beside it. That's the map pack, and for a plumber, cafe, electrician or physio it's frequently the single highest-value position in all of search — because the people looking at it are ready to call someone right now.

Which three businesses appear is decided largely by your Google Business Profile: how complete it is, how it's categorised, how close you are to the searcher, and what your reviews look like.

Why you might not be showing up

  • The profile was never claimed or never verified
  • The primary category is wrong — this is extremely common and heavily weighted
  • Business details don't match what's on your website
  • Too few reviews relative to the businesses currently ranking
  • Services and attributes left blank, so Google can't match you to searches
  • No photos, or three photos uploaded in 2019
  • You're simply further from the searcher than the businesses shown

Most of those are fixable in an afternoon. Proximity isn't — but a stronger profile widens the radius over which you can still compete.

On buying reviews

Don't, and I won't do it for you. Purchased reviews violate Google's policies, and enforcement has become considerably better at detecting them. The downside is a suspended profile, which means vanishing from the map entirely — a far worse position than the one you were trying to improve on. A simple system for asking real customers at the right moment outperforms it anyway, and it compounds.

Start here if money is tight. If you can only afford to fix one thing, fix this. It costs nothing to claim, it's usually the fastest source of new calls for a local business, and it works even if your website is average.
